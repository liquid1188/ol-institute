Orientale Lumen InstituteA word from our founder

Dear friends,

When I started the Orientale Lumen Conference, Pope John Paul’s apostolic letter Orientale Lumen was not getting a lot of attention. Over dinner I proposed an open-to-the-public meeting about it to Bishop Kallistos. He immediately agreed, got his diary out, and we planned the first conference for June 1997. About a hundred people came.

Ever since, we have tried to keep a balance: prayer for the soul, lectures for the mind, and fellowship for the spirit. Eastern Orthodox, Eastern Catholics and Roman Catholics meet and pray together and learn from each other’s traditions, trying to obey Christ’s command, “that they all may be one,” for which he prayed at the Last Supper.

The official dialogue has made real progress, with the agreed statements of Ravenna (2007), Chieti (2016), and Alexandria (2023). The Institute is how we carry our “grass roots” effort into the classroom, to equip a younger generation of ecumenically minded, faith-based people to receive those agreements and carry them forward.

Full communion is my own personal goal to see, hopefully in my lifetime. It is going to be a miracle, and it is going to be on God’s time. Until then we can learn, pray, and become friends. Thank you for spreading the light from the East to the West.

Yours in Christ,Jack FigelJack FigelFounder, Orientale Lumen Foundation

One Church, two calendars

Counting the days to Pascha, East and West

The Catholic Church and many Orthodox Churches keep Christmas on December 25 of the civil calendar. The Churches of Jerusalem, Russia, Serbia, and Georgia, and the monasteries of Mount Athos, keep the older Julian calendar, now thirteen days behind. Nearly all Orthodox reckon Pascha by the Julian calendar, so East and West keep the feast on the same Sunday only in some years.

Today is October 15 on the Gregorian calendar and October 2 on the Julian calendar.

Gregorian calendar

Kept by Roman Catholics and by Eastern Catholics such as the Maronites

Our parent organization

The Orientale Lumen Foundation

Started in 1997 in Washington, DC, these ecumenical conferences are a “grass roots” movement among lay persons and clergy to provide a forum for Christians to learn about the “light from the east.” They allow Eastern Orthodox, Eastern Catholics and Roman Catholics to meet and pray together, learn from each other’s traditions, and become friends together searching for a common goal: “that they all may be one” in the One Church of Christ.

The Institute carries that work into the classroom. Gifts to its Founding Campaign endow a chair in ecumenical studies and pay for its first teachers and classes.
