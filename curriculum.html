Master of Arts in Ecumenical Studies

Sample curriculum

A draft of what two years in the MA could look like: twelve courses, each read with Catholic and Orthodox teachers side by side.

Draft for discussion. This sample curriculum is a proposal for the Institute’s founders and Academic Senate to revise. Course numbers, titles, and readings will change.

Length
2 years
Credits
36 credit hours, 12 courses of 3
Conference
A seat at the Orientale Lumen Conference each summer
Readings
Catholic and Orthodox authors in every course

Year One Fall

ECU 501 · 3 credits

Foundations of Ecumenism

Why the churches seek unity, told from both sides: Rome’s commitment at Vatican II and Constantinople’s call to the churches a generation earlier.

Core readings

  • Ecumenical Patriarchate, Unto the Churches of Christ Everywhere (1920)
  • Second Vatican Council, Unitatis Redintegratio and Orientalium Ecclesiarum (1964)
  • Pope Paul VI and Ecumenical Patriarch Athenagoras, Joint Catholic-Orthodox Declaration lifting the anathemas of 1054 (1965)
  • John Paul II, Orientale Lumen and Ut Unum Sint (1995)
  • Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church (Crete), Relations of the Orthodox Church with the Rest of the Christian World (2016)
  • World Council of Churches, Faith and Order, Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry (1982), written with Catholic and Orthodox theologians
  • Jaroslav Pelikan, The Vindication of Tradition (Yale, 1984): “Tradition is the living faith of the dead; traditionalism is the dead faith of the living”

Further reading

  • Yves Congar, After Nine Hundred Years (Fordham, 1959)
  • Olivier Clément, Dialogues with Patriarch Athenagoras (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Sister Churches 101 (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Walter Kasper, Harvesting the Fruits: Basic Aspects of Christian Faith in Ecumenical Dialogue (Continuum, 2009)

ECU 510 · 3 credits

The Christian East

The Orthodox Church as Orthodox teachers present it: its faith, worship, and life.

Core readings

  • Kallistos Ware, The Orthodox Church: An Introduction to Eastern Christianity (Penguin, new ed. 2015)
  • John Meyendorff, Byzantine Theology: Historical Trends and Doctrinal Themes (Fordham, 1974)
  • Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew, Encountering the Mystery: Understanding Orthodox Christianity Today (Doubleday, 2008)
  • Jaroslav Pelikan, The Spirit of Eastern Christendom (600–1700) (Chicago, 1974)

Further reading

  • Holy Tradition (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Georges Florovsky, Bible, Church, Tradition: An Eastern Orthodox View (1972)
  • Andrew Louth, Modern Orthodox Thinkers: From the Philokalia to the Present (2015)

ECU 515 · 3 credits

The Catholic Church and the Latin West

The Catholic Church as Catholic teachers present it, so Orthodox and Catholic students meet each church on its own terms.

Core readings

  • Catechism of the Catholic Church, Part One, on the creed and the Church
  • Second Vatican Council, Lumen Gentium (1964)
  • Joseph Ratzinger, Introduction to Christianity
  • Henri de Lubac, Catholicism: Christ and the Common Destiny of Man

Further reading

  • Henri de Lubac, The Splendor of the Church
  • Joseph Ratzinger, Principles of Catholic Theology, including the 1976 Graz lecture behind the “Ratzinger Formula”

Year One Spring

ECU 520 · 3 credits

The Shared Fathers

The common inheritance of the first millennium, read with a teacher from each tradition.

Core readings

  • Athanasius, On the Incarnation; Gregory of Nazianzus, Theological Orations; Augustine, Confessions
  • Maximus the Confessor, Selected Writings, trans. George C. Berthold (Classics of Western Spirituality, Paulist, 1985), a Greek father who suffered for the faith and was defended by Rome
  • Kallistos Ware, Lectures from Oxford, Volume I: Patristics (Eastern Christian Publications)

Further reading

  • Hans Urs von Balthasar, Cosmic Liturgy: The Universe According to Maximus the Confessor

ECU 530 · 3 credits

The Great Schism and the Wounds of Memory

How East and West drifted apart, what divided them, and the injuries each still remembers, including the sack of Constantinople in 1204.

Core readings

  • Henry Chadwick, East and West: The Making of a Rift in the Church (Oxford, 2003)
  • A. Edward Siecienski, Beards, Azymes, and Purgatory: The Other Issues that Divided East and West (Oxford, 2022)
  • John H. Erickson, The Challenge of Our Past: Studies in Orthodox Canon Law and Church History (St Vladimir’s Seminary Press, 1991)
  • James Likoudis, Ending the Byzantine Greek Schism (Emmaus Road, 3rd ed. 2026, foreword by Scott Hahn)
  • John Paul II, address in Athens asking forgiveness for 1204 (May 4, 2001)

Further reading

  • Jonathan Harris, Byzantium and the Crusades
  • Constantinople, Rome and the Churches of Greece (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Tia M. Kolbaba, The Byzantine Lists: Errors of the Latins (Illinois, 2000)

ECU 540 · 3 credits

The Trinity: Filioque, Essence, and Energies

The two great theological questions, one pressed by the East and one by the West, each read in its own sources.

Core readings

  • A. Edward Siecienski, The Filioque: History of a Doctrinal Controversy (Oxford, 2010)
  • Maximus the Confessor, Letter to Marinus, his defense of the Latin teaching on the procession of the Spirit
  • Pontifical Council for Promoting Christian Unity, The Greek and the Latin Traditions regarding the Procession of the Holy Spirit (1995)
  • North American Orthodox-Catholic Theological Consultation, The Filioque: A Church-Dividing Issue? (2003)
  • Gregory Palamas, The Triads, ed. John Meyendorff (Classics of Western Spirituality, Paulist, 1983)

Further reading

  • Vladimir Lossky, The Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church (1957)
  • Aristotle Papanikolaou, Being with God: Trinity, Apophaticism, and Divine-Human Communion (Notre Dame, 2006)

Year Two Fall

ECU 550 · 3 credits

Primacy and Synodality: The Debate

The question at the heart of the dialogue, read as a debate: the strongest case from each church, the history between them, and what the churches have signed together.

Core readings

  • The Catholic case, from history. Klaus Schatz, SJ, Papal Primacy: From Its Origins to the Present, trans. John A. Otto and Linda M. Maloney (Liturgical Press, 1996); Francis Dvornik, Byzantium and the Roman Primacy (Fordham, 1966); First Vatican Council, Pastor Aeternus (1870)
  • The Orthodox case. John Meyendorff, ed., The Primacy of Peter: Essays in Ecclesiology and the Early Church (St Vladimir’s Seminary Press); Moscow Patriarchate, Position on the Problem of Primacy in the Universal Church (2013); John Zizioulas, Being as Communion (St Vladimir’s Seminary Press, 1985)
  • The history between them. A. Edward Siecienski, The Papacy and the Orthodox: Sources and History of a Debate (Oxford, 2017); George E. Demacopoulos, The Invention of Peter: Apostolic Discourse and Papal Authority in Late Antiquity (Penn, 2013)
  • Bridges. Olivier Clément, You Are Peter (New City, 2003); Adam A. J. DeVille, Orthodoxy and the Roman Papacy (Notre Dame, 2011)
  • What the churches have signed. Ecumenical Documents on Primacy: Ravenna, Chieti, and Alexandria (Eastern Christian Publications); Dicastery for Promoting Christian Unity, The Bishop of Rome (2024)

Further reading

  • Erick Ybarra, The Papacy: Revisiting the Debate Between Catholics and Orthodox (Emmaus Road, 2023), the fullest recent Catholic argument from the first-millennium sources
  • James Likoudis, The Divine Primacy of the Bishop of Rome and Modern Eastern Orthodoxy: Letters to a Greek Orthodox on the Unity of the Church (Emmaus Road, 2023)
  • Nicholas Afanasiev, The Church of the Holy Spirit (Notre Dame, 2007)
  • Paul McPartlan, The Eucharist Makes the Church (Eastern Christian Publications, 2nd ed.)
  • Richard A. Mattiussi, The Ratzinger Formula (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Hans Urs von Balthasar, The Office of Peter and the Structure of the Church

ECU 610 · 3 credits

Eastern Catholics, Uniatism, and Kyiv

Churches of the East in communion with Rome, the Union of Brest, and why the Orthodox call uniatism the sorest point in the dialogue.

Core readings

  • Articles of the Union of Brest (1595–96)
  • Peter Mohila, The Orthodox Confession of Faith (1640, approved at the Synod of Jassy, 1642), from the Orthodox Metropolitan of Kyiv in the generation after Brest
  • Joint International Commission, Balamand Statement, Uniatism, Method of Union of the Past, and the Present Search for Full Communion (1993)
  • Cyril Korolevsky, Uniatism, trans. Serge Keleher (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Vatican Documents on the Eastern Churches, Volume 1 (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Maximos IV Sayegh, ed., The Eastern Churches and Catholic Unity (1963), Melkite bishops speaking for themselves at the eve of Vatican II

Further reading

  • Ines Angeli Murzaku, Returning Home to Rome? The Monks of Grottaferrata in Albania (2009)
  • James Likoudis, Heralds of a Catholic Russia: Twelve Spiritual Pilgrims from Byzantium to Rome, profiles of converts (Blue Army Press, 2023)
  • Metropolitan Andrey Sheptytsky, selected pastoral letters
  • Jaroslav Pelikan and Valerie Hotchkiss, eds., Creeds and Confessions of Faith in the Christian Tradition (Yale, 2003), with Mohila’s Orthodox Confession and the other Eastern confessions in English

ECU 620 · 3 credits

Liturgy and the Spiritual Life

How each tradition prays and seeks holiness, and the monks and saints both churches share.

Core readings

  • Alexander Schmemann, For the Life of the World (St Vladimir’s Seminary Press)
  • Robert Taft, SJ, Beyond East and West: Problems in Liturgical Understanding (Pontifical Oriental Institute, 2nd ed. 1997)
  • Kallistos Ware, The Orthodox Way (St Vladimir’s Seminary Press)
  • Tomáš Špidlík, The Spirituality of the Christian East: A Systematic Handbook (Cistercian, 1986)
  • Raymond L. Capra, Ines Angeli Murzaku, and Douglas J. Milewski, trans., The Life of Saint Neilos of Rossano (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library, 2018)

Further reading

  • Kallistos Ware, Lectures from Oxford, Volumes IV: Liturgy and V: Spirituality (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Robert Taft, SJ, Lectures from Rome (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Barbara Crostini and Ines Angeli Murzaku, eds., Greek Monasticism in Southern Italy: The Life of Neilos in Context (Routledge)
  • Easter Together: An Ecumenical Exploration for a Common Date, Orientale Lumen XXVI (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Rowan Williams, Looking East in Winter: Contemporary Thought and the Eastern Christian Tradition (2021)

Year Two Spring

ECU 640 · 3 credits

Councils of Reunion and Why They Failed

Lyons and Florence from both sides: those who signed, those who refused, and why the East did not receive them.

Core readings

  • Selected acts of the Second Council of Lyons (1274) and the Council of Florence (1439)
  • Mark of Ephesus, Encyclical Letter to All Orthodox Christians (1440), from the bishop who refused to sign at Florence
  • Deno John Geanakoplos, Byzantine East and Latin West (1966)
  • Aidan Nichols, OP, Rome and the Eastern Churches (Ignatius, 2nd ed. 2010)
  • James Likoudis, Scholars of the Sacred: Dominican Theologians in Late Medieval Byzantium (En Route, 2023)

Further reading

  • Joseph Gill, SJ, The Council of Florence (Cambridge, 1959)
  • Kallistos Ware, Lectures from Oxford, Volume VI: History (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Marcus Plested, Orthodox Readings of Aquinas (Oxford, 2012)

ECU 650 · 3 credits

Every Agreed Statement

The whole official dialogue with the Orthodox, read in order, and the Christological agreements with the Oriental churches, so every graduate has read every agreed text.

Core readings

  • Joint International Commission: Munich, The Mystery of the Church and of the Eucharist in the Light of the Mystery of the Holy Trinity (1982); Bari, Faith, Sacraments and the Unity of the Church (1987); Valamo, The Sacrament of Order in the Sacramental Structure of the Church (1988)
  • Balamand (1993); Ravenna, Ecclesiological and Canonical Consequences of the Sacramental Nature of the Church (2007); Chieti, Synodality and Primacy During the First Millennium (2016); Alexandria, Synodality and Primacy in the Second Millennium and Today (2023)
  • North American Orthodox-Catholic Theological Consultation, The Journey Toward Unity, Volume I (Eastern Christian Publications), and its statement Steps Towards a Reunited Church (2010)
  • Kallistos Ware, Lectures from Oxford, Volume III: Ecumenism (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • The Oriental Churches. Pope Paul VI and Pope Shenouda III, Common Declaration (1973); John Paul II and Patriarch Mar Dinkha IV, Common Christological Declaration with the Assyrian Church of the East (1994); International Joint Commission between the Catholic Church and the Oriental Orthodox Churches, Nature, Constitution and Mission of the Church (2009)

Further reading

  • Toward a Theology of Reconciliation, an Orthodox perspective (Eastern Christian Publications)
  • Selected proceedings of the Orientale Lumen Conferences (Eastern Christian Publications)

ECU 690 · 3 credits

Capstone

A thesis, or a practicum built around the Orientale Lumen Conference or an Institute pilgrimage.

Core readings

  • Readings set with the student’s advisor

Electives Languages

Optional, not required for the degree. Recommended for students who plan to read the Fathers and the councils in the original or go on to doctoral work.

LAN 501–502 · 3 credits each

New Testament and Patristic Greek

Two semesters of grammar and reading, from the Gospels to the Cappadocians and Maximus.

LAN 511–512 · 3 credits each

Ecclesiastical Latin

Two semesters of grammar and reading, from the Vulgate and Augustine to the acts of Florence and the documents of the councils.

Each church is read in its own voice: official texts from both sides, the strongest case each makes on the hard questions, and the history between them. Students meet the Orthodox Church through Orthodox teachers and the Catholic Church through Catholic teachers, and by graduation every student has read every agreed statement of the international Catholic-Orthodox dialogue. Titles marked Eastern Christian Publications are available from ecpubs.com. The reading list should be reviewed with the Institute’s Orthodox and Catholic advisers before it is final.
